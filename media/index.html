MEDIA ART CATEGORIES

Case Study — Typology Research Board
01. Video & Film Art
Bill Viola · Pipilotti Rist · Nam June Paik · Hannah Weinberger

Video art emerged in the 1960s as artists seized the camera as a sculptural and narrative tool. From Nam June Paik's pioneering television assemblages to Bill Viola's slow, immersive meditations on mortality, and Pipilotti Rist's lush psychedelic video environments, this category treats moving image not as entertainment but as a material to bend, loop, and layer.

The monitor and projection screen become canvases for consciousness. Hannah Weinberger extends this into multichannel sound-video installations that collapse the boundary between film, music, and lived space.

02. Immersive & Light Installation
James Turrell · Olafur Eliasson · Ryoji Ikeda

Light itself is the medium here — not as illumination but as phenomenological event. James Turrell carves pure light into architectural chambers where the eye loses its anchor. Olafur Eliasson scales natural phenomena — fog, rainbows, the sun — into museum-sized experiences that make perception conscious.

Ryoji Ikeda works at the intersection of pure data and light, translating binary information into overwhelming white projections and precise sound. Together they share an interest in how spatial light restructures the observer's relationship to scale, time, and sensory threshold.

03. AI & Generative Art
Refik Anadol · Mario Klingemann · Ian Cheng

This category encompasses artists who use machine learning and generative systems as primary creative partners. Refik Anadol feeds vast datasets — architectural history, nature, human memory — into neural networks and renders the output as fluid, monumental projections that redefine what data visualization can look like.

Mario Klingemann trains models on cultural archives to produce uncanny, glitchy imagery probing the aesthetics of algorithmic thought. Ian Cheng builds self-evolving simulations — living game-like worlds — that run continuously without authorial control, producing emergent narratives no human scripted.

04. Performance & Body Art
Laurie Anderson · Sougwen Chung · Yayoi Kusama

Here the body — human or hybrid — is both instrument and subject. Laurie Anderson has spent decades using electronic voice manipulation, storytelling, and multimedia performance to examine language, identity, and technology. Sougwen Chung performs alongside robotic arms she trained on her own drawing behavior, staging a live negotiation between biological and artificial mark-making.

Yayoi Kusama's obsessive dot-pattern performances and infinity mirror rooms stem from a deeply personal visual language, turning self-obliteration into an aesthetic system of radical repetition.

05. Sound Art
Janet Cardiff · Ryoji Ikeda · Hannah Weinberger · Laurie Anderson

Sound art decouples audio from performance or playback, treating it as spatial, architectural, or sculptural material. Janet Cardiff's binaural audio walks embed fictional narratives into real environments, collapsing memory and place. Ryoji Ikeda uses sine waves and noise at extreme frequencies as structural elements.

Hannah Weinberger fills rooms with layered multichannel sound that behaves more like weather than music. Laurie Anderson's voice — electronically altered, looped, stretched — becomes an instrument for dissecting language itself. What unites them: sound is the primary structural fact, not decoration.

06. New Media & Net Art
Stan VanDerBeek · Nam June Paik · Gala Porras-Kim

New media art exploits the specific properties of electronic and network-based systems — early video synthesis, satellite broadcasts, internet infrastructure, database logic. Stan VanDerBeek invented the 'movie-drome' in the 1960s, a dome-shaped cinema for image saturation. Nam June Paik envisioned a global electronic superhighway decades before the internet.

Gala Porras-Kim uses archival, bureaucratic, and institutional systems as her medium, interrogating how cultural memory is preserved and lost within protocols. The category is defined by an interest in the cultural politics of media infrastructure itself.

07. Sculpture & Mixed Media
Cai Guo-Qiang · Anicka Yi · Cao Fei

These artists work across physical materials, biological processes, and digital environments without privileging any single one. Cai Guo-Qiang uses gunpowder — ignited in controlled explosions — to produce monumental drawings and pyrotechnic events merging spectacle with ritual. Anicka Yi builds immersive installations using scent, biotechnology, and robotics, exploring embodiment and non-human intelligence through microbiology.

Cao Fei operates across documentary film, virtual worlds, and installation, building a sprawling fictional universe that mirrors China's rapid urbanization. The category is united by a commitment to material hybridity as conceptual argument.

08. Architecture & Spatial Media
Doug Aitken · Olafur Eliasson · Refik Anadol

This category addresses the built environment as a site of media projection, transformation, and experience. Doug Aitken wraps buildings in large-scale video projections and constructs mirrored pavilions that fragment landscape into infinite reflection, treating architecture as a screen.

Olafur Eliasson collaborates with architects to embed light, climate, and ecological phenomena into buildings and public spaces. Refik Anadol projects machine-generated imagery across building facades at urban scale, recasting architecture as a dynamic data surface — dissolving the boundary between structure and image.
