DS1201 2GBX

Project Brief Mapping
01. Studio Concept

The studio approaches architecture through the convergence of typology, technology, and ecology[cite: 27]. These are not treated as fixed categories but as interrelated architectural questions that shape how buildings are conceived, organized, and realized[cite: 28].

Underlying the 2GBX studio is an interest in transforming environmental challenges into opportunities for innovation and creative response[cite: 23]. The studio aims to reconceptualize the relationship between humans and the natural world, with the goal of contributing to a more resilient built environment[cite: 25].

02. What Are We Designing?

Students will design a Media Museum[cite: 31]. The project is an open-ended typology rather than a fixed institutional model[cite: 91].

The building type is formulated through architectural decisions about new user patterns, spatial/functional hybrids, and the project's typo-morphological relationship to its urban context[cite: 31]. The Media Museum is approached as a framework that can accommodate evolving forms of media production, exhibition, storage, and public engagement, rather than as a singular or static container[cite: 92].

03. Where Are We Designing?

The project is sited on a 173,000 sf surface parking lot within Hudson Yards, New York City[cite: 36, 76]. Students will work with the full site while developing a building with a maximum total area of approximately 300,000 sf[cite: 77]. This ends up allowing for a majority of the site to be open space[cite: 78].

Hudson Yards represents a highly mediated urban environment - one shaped by coordinated planning, privatized public space, infrastructural concentration, and image management at the scale of the district[cite: 37].

04. Studio Aims

The course introduces students to tools at the forefront of digital design to develop formal techniques, aesthetic sensibilities, and new modes of digital project representation that advance architectural thinking[cite: 20].

Students will develop a comprehensive understanding of principles central to architectural practice and will have the opportunity to engage deeply with disciplinary issues[cite: 22].

Students develop architecture that operates within this entanglement, with massing, envelope, and spatial organization responding to urban, climatic, and infrastructural forces[cite: 40].

05. Theoretical Discourse

Typology is understood as provisional and open-ended, particularly in contemporary cultural buildings where program, use, and spatial organization are actively being re-defined[cite: 30].

Technology is instrumental, not a background system. Students will engage media installation systems, building structure, and representational tools (including Unreal Engine) as active components of architectural authorship, shaping how the building is assembled, organized, and perceived[cite: 32].

Ecology is framed as an unavoidable condition of architectural production, not as an ethical overlay[cite: 33]. The studio positions architectural form, envelope, and organization as ways of acting deliberately within those conditions, not to resolve them but rather to entangle with these conditions in new ways[cite: 34].
